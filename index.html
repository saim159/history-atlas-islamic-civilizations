Chronology

A Brief Historical Timeline

A selection of milestones to help orient further research. Each date should be read in its specific historical context.

c. 570 CE

Birth of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Muhammad was born in Mecca in western Arabia, a city connected to regional pilgrimage and trade networks.

610 CE (traditional dating)

Beginning of Revelation

Islamic tradition dates the beginning of Qur'anic revelation to Muhammad's retreat near Mecca.

622 CE

Hijra to Madinah

The migration to Madinah became the starting point of the Islamic Hijri calendar and a turning point in the early Muslim community.

661 CE

Beginning of Umayyad Rule

Damascus became the centre of an expanding caliphate whose political and cultural influence reached across multiple regions.

750 CE

Abbasid Revolution

The Abbasids replaced Umayyad rule in most of the caliphate; Baghdad was founded later, in 762 CE.

1258 CE

Mongol Capture of Baghdad

The capture and devastation of Baghdad marked a major rupture in Abbasid political history, but scholarship and Muslim societies continued elsewhere.

1453 CE

Ottoman Conquest of Constantinople

The Ottoman conquest transformed the city's political role and contributed to a new phase in regional imperial history.

1526 CE

Beginning of Mughal Rule in North India

Babur's victory at Panipat laid the foundation for the Mughal Empire, which developed diverse political and cultural institutions.

Key terminology

A Short Historical Glossary

Caliphate — খিলাফত

A political institution associated with leadership of the Muslim community; its forms changed over time.

Hijra — হিজরত

The migration of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and early Muslims from Mecca to Madinah in 622 CE.

Waqf — ওয়াক্‌ফ

A charitable endowment that supported religious, educational, and social purposes in many Muslim societies.

Madrasah — মাদরাসা

An educational institution whose curricula and social roles varied across time and place.

Isnad — ইসনাদ

A chain of transmitters associated with the transmission and evaluation of hadith reports.

Historiography — ইতিহাসচর্চার পদ্ধতি

The study of how history is written, interpreted, evidenced, and debated.

Research methodology

Think Like a Historian

Good history is built through evidence, comparison, context, and careful interpretation—not simply by collecting dates and famous names.

01

Ask a Focused Question

Instead of asking whether a civilisation was great, ask how a specific institution, city, or network changed over time.

02

Check the Evidence

Distinguish contemporary documents from later chronicles. Examine authorship, purpose, audience, date, and possible bias.

03

Compare Interpretations

Read multiple credible historians, note disagreement, and explain why interpretations differ.
