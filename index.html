A broad chronology

History across time

Dates below are orientation points. Period boundaries and political transitions differ by region and are sometimes debated.

c. 600–632 CE

Prophetic period

Revelation, community formation and the early Muslim polity.

632–750 CE

Early caliphates

Rashidun and Umayyad periods; rapid political expansion.

750–1258 CE

Abbasid era

A long, changing period of scholarship, regional powers and cultural exchange.

c. 700–1492 CE

Islamic Iberia

Different polities in al-Andalus, ending with Granada’s conquest in 1492.

c. 1200–1924 CE

Later empires

Mongol transformations, Mamluks, Ottomans and South Asian empires.

Primary texts

Texts, editions & translations

Record author, title, editor/translator, publisher, year and page. Compare editions and distinguish contemporary evidence from later narrative.

Internet Archive ↗
Citation standard: do not invent page numbers. For books such as Mawlana Ismail Rehan’s works, add volume and page only after checking the exact edition. Label contested accounts as contested and distinguish religious tradition, later chronicles and independently corroborated evidence.
Key terminology

A small historical glossary

Terms can vary by period and author. These short definitions are starting points for careful reading.

Khilāfah — الخلافةCaliphate; a political-religious institution whose form changed across history.
Bayʿah — البيعةA pledge of allegiance; meaning and practice depended on context.
Diwan — الديوانAn administrative register or bureau in various Muslim states.
Waqf — الوقفA charitable or religious endowment governed by specific legal traditions.
Isnād — الإسنادA chain of transmitters, especially important in hadith scholarship.
HistoriographyThe study of how history is written, interpreted and debated.
Our research principles

History deserves curiosity and care.

History Atlas aims to make the Muslim past easier to explore without flattening its complexity. We distinguish documented events, later narratives, scholarly interpretations and unresolved debates.

  • Cite the edition actually consulted.
  • Avoid fabricated quotations, page numbers or certainty.
  • Represent disagreement fairly and respectfully.
